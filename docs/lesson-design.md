# Lines & Angles, Class 2: lesson design

This document is for the designers and educators working on Driftbound. It describes the lesson that
students take before the game: where its content comes from, how each step works, and how it
connects to the game.

## Source

The content is the deck *Angles Learning section.pdf* (43 slides, "Lines & Angles · Class 2"). The
captions on screen are the deck's own sentences, in the deck's order. Nothing is taught that the deck
does not teach.

Skills covered (deck slide 1): explain and identify adjacent angles, linear pairs and vertically
opposite angles, then solve missing-angle problems.

## Setting

It opens on a start banner: the satellite HQ in orbit above a planet, with one play button in the
centre. Pressing play (which also lets the browser start the voice) plays the intro, about 20 seconds,
one continuous camera move with no cuts:

1. The camera flies into the satellite, faster and faster, and arrives at its entry gate.
2. The gate checks in: its two lights blink, a scan sweeps down the doors, the lights turn green, the
   latches let go and the doors slide apart under the frame, with light and a puff of air.
3. The camera walks in at eye height (a step bob, a slight sway and roll, a footstep on each step):
   through the gate, down the corridor and through its far doorway into the command hall. The gate,
   the corridor and the hall each stand at their own distance, so near things grow faster than far
   ones, and the walk stops exactly on the lesson's view of the hall.
4. It looks all the way round the hall (a full turn), slowing at each wall: the surveillance
   monitors, the entrance it came through, the map table, and back to the front wall, where the
   DRIFTBOUND hologram rises from a projector table (there is no screen yet).
5. A drone flies in and projects the screen; the screen forms top to bottom like a hologram while the
   hall's lights go down, and the drone flies off. Then the lesson starts.

Skip (or Escape) goes straight to the lesson; with reduced motion the intro is left out.

The lesson plays in the command HQ inside the ship's satellite: a calm, domed hall with a window band
onto the planet (a picture made with Figma AI). In front of it is one big, rectangular hologram
screen that fills most of the window: a glowing edge, a faint honeycomb grid and a slow scan line. The
whole lesson plays on that screen: the caption bar at the top, the diagram in the middle, the deck's
sums and labels at the bottom. Back, the answers and Next sit in one row under the screen. On phones
the answers sit above Back and Next.

The narration is one line at the top of the screen, beside the AI mouth (the voice indicator). It is a
recorded voice, the game's co-pilot (Kokoro's af_heart voice with a light robot sound). Each word
lights up at the moment the voice says it, and the mouth's bars follow the voice. The line shows one sentence
at a time: an instruction (for example "Drag B.") is said after the caption and then takes the line,
so a question and a task never run together; a long line comes in short pieces, one after another (a
piece that goes on with the sentence joins it); a wrong-answer hint takes the line while it is said,
then the caption or instruction comes back. Answer buttons appear once the question has been said. There is no
character, no progress bar and no other text.

## Interactions and animations

- Lines and rays draw out from their starting point; arm OB turns away from OA so an angle reads as a turn.
- The student only taps or drags where it means something: answering (buttons, cards, or the angle on
  the diagram), and exploring (drag C: the two angles change but always make 180°; turn a line:
  opposite angles stay equal, all four values live). Everything else plays by itself:
  - Measuring: a holo protractor sweeps its needle across each angle while the number counts up.
  - Adjacent angles: the shared vertex rings, the shared arm lights up, the two angles blink in turn.
  - The half turn: the edge of a linear pair lights up from one side of the line to the other while the
    size counts up to 180°.
  - Turning: a copy of ∠1 turns half a turn round the vertex and lies exactly on ∠3.
  - Joining: the 110° angle slides onto the 70° angle; their outer arms snap into one line.
  - The proof: each line of the working follows the one before once it has been said.
- Card questions: on the right card the others fade away and it glides to the middle. When the next idea
  uses the card's figure (the complementary pair, which then shows adjacent angles; the supplementary
  pair, which then forms a linear pair), the card's frame goes and the figure stays: it grows to fill the
  screen and carries on.
- Praise: every right answer and every finished hands-on task gets a short spoken line ("Great job!",
  "Well done!"...; "You got it!" after a miss), then a few words on what was right ("30° + 60° = 90°.",
  "Acute: less than 90°.", "∠3 faces ∠1 across O."), as one line in the narration bar.
- Key words: in the narration line, the terms the lesson teaches and every angle size are gold.
- Dragging: point C and the crossing line can be dragged; the numbers update live.
- Each step materializes on the screen from top to bottom, and the scan line sweeps across it.
- Ghost animation: words, buttons, cards and labels come in see-through and blurred, drift up and
  turn solid.
- Drag knobs: a glowing ring with two curved arrows either side, along the way it turns. Until the
  first drag the arrows pop in and nudge, and the pointing hand rests on the knob and presses it, with
  a ripple (it never travels). The ray's arrowhead gives way to the knob.
- Joining two angles: the pair glides to the middle of its card and grows to fill it.
- Right answers burst into small glowing hexagons.

## Additions beyond the deck

Kept to what the interactions need:

1. The instruction for each task (one short line in the caption bar, read aloud).
2. Explanations for wrong answers (the specific mistake: the given angle, using 90° instead of 180°,
   adjacent instead of opposite, and so on).
3. The start banner with its play button, and Launch mission on the last step, which opens the game.

## How it maps to the game

| Lesson | In the game |
|---|---|
| Linear pair (steps 5–9) | Levels L1, L3, L4, L6, L8 and the mixed mastery levels: x = 180° − the given angle |
| Vertically opposite angles (steps 13–18) | Levels L2, L5, L7 and the mastery levels: x = the given angle |

## Accessibility

- Keyboard: → or Enter for Next, ← for Back, Space to finish or skip a spoken line, Tab to reach
  every button. The two handles (C and the crossing line) take the arrow keys.
- Screen readers: captions and instructions are announced; handles are sliders with their value.
- Colour is never the only cue: every angle carries a name or a number.
- Reduced motion turns off the screen effects, tweens and bursts. The sound button turns the voice off.

## Production notes

- The voice-over is made with Kokoro (free, open source, runs locally) by `tools/vo/make_vo.py`, in the
  game's co-pilot voice; each line stores its word timings.
- The intro's gate (cut into its frame and two door halves), corridor (its far doorway cut open) and
  the hall's right, back and left walls (stitched with the front into one strip for the full turn) are
  Figma AI pictures (`assets/ui/intro/`).
- The start banner, title, play button, recall drone, recall card and hint hand are Figma AI pictures
  (`assets/ui/start/`, `assets/ui/recall/`).
- The HQ background is two pictures made with Figma AI (`assets/ui/bg/`): a wide one for landscape
  screens and a tall one for phones. `src/engine/scene.js` places the lesson screen on the front wall,
  where the pictures had a screen; `tools/ui/hall_wall.py` paints that screen out and adds the
  DRIFTBOUND hologram and its projector table.
- The buttons are the capsules from the POLYGON Figma file (source art in `tools/ui/polygon/`).
- The UI kit is in Figma (https://www.figma.com/design/tyeZIE8dPEVxyAIccfBrov). Its screens show an
  earlier layout.
- Diagrams are SVG, drawn by `src/engine/figure.js`, so they stay sharp at any size.
- No build step: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules.
