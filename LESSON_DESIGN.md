# Driftbound Briefing: lesson design

This document is for the designers and educators working on Driftbound. It describes the angle lesson that students take before they play the game: what it teaches, how each scene works, and how every part connects to the game.

## Purpose

Driftbound is a space shooter where the cannon turns by an angle the student chooses. To aim, the student has to find an unknown angle x. The briefing teaches the angle facts the game relies on, in the game's own world, so that students start the first level already knowing the rules, the notation and how to read the radar.

The lesson takes place at a strategy meeting on the ship. The co-pilot robot runs the meeting, the Warden appears as a hologram (the threat the student will face at the end of the game), and a raider (GLIB) shows up during practice to taunt the student.

The lesson has 10 scenes and takes about 8 to 12 minutes. An optional extra-training scene covers three more angle facts.

## Audience and prerequisites

- Students aged about 10 to 13 (grades 5 to 7), playing on a phone, tablet or computer.
- They need to read whole numbers up to 360 and to add and subtract two- and three-digit numbers.
- They do not need to know anything about measuring angles beforehand.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the briefing, the student can:

1. Describe an angle as the amount of turn between two lines that meet at a point.
2. Measure turns in degrees, using the landmarks 90° (right angle), 180° (straight line) and 360° (full turn).
3. Name acute, right, obtuse, straight and reflex angles.
4. Find an unknown angle on a straight line: x = 180° − the given angle (Rule 1).
5. Find an unknown vertically opposite angle: x = the given angle (Rule 2).
6. Recognise the common wrong answers, and check an answer by estimating first and then checking the sum.
7. Read the game's battle radar: the cannon beam, the enemy line, the given angle, x and the timer.

## Shared language with the game

The lesson uses exactly what the game uses, so nothing needs to be relearned:

| Element | In the game and in the lesson |
|---|---|
| Cannon beam | Bright cyan line from the ship's cannon; the reference line for x |
| Enemy line | Red line from the cannon to the enemy |
| Extensions | Dotted lines that continue a line through the cannon (a straight line, or the second line of a crossing) |
| Given angle | Orange arc with its number |
| Unknown angle | White arc labelled with an italic serif x |
| Right angle | A small white square in the corner |
| Radar | Thin cyan ring with a tick every 10° and a longer tick every 30° |
| Question bar | "Find x" in gold, the question in white, a time bar along the bottom |
| Answers | Gold capsule buttons that turn green when right and red when wrong |
| Feedback | The rule on a miss, the second hint after another miss, the working on a hit |
| Characters | The co-pilot (teacher), the Warden (boss), raiders (enemies), each with their portrait and comms box |

## Scene by scene

Each scene has a short title, one interaction and a few co-pilot lines. Lines type out in the comms box; tapping the box finishes a line or skips to the next one. Next is always available, and it pulses once the scene's point has landed, so no student gets stuck.

| # | Scene | What the student does | Co-pilot script (main lines) | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strategy meeting | Watches the Warden's hologram over the holo-table; taps Start | "Welcome to the strategy meeting, pilot." Warden: "Nobody gets past the Warden!" "Our cannon turns by angles. Find x to aim." | The lines finish |
| 2 | What is an angle? | Drags the cannon beam around the radar; the white arc and its number follow | "An angle is how far a line turns." "Drag the cannon beam around the radar." "Bigger turn, bigger angle!" | The beam has been turned |
| 3 | Degrees | Taps 90°, 180° and 360°; the beam turns to each landmark | "We measure turns in degrees." "A quarter turn is 90°: a right angle. See the little square?" "A half turn is 180°. It makes a straight line." "A full turn is 360°. All the way round." | All three landmarks tapped |
| 4 | Angle types | Names five angles (35°, 90°, 128°, 180°, 245°, in random order) | "Every angle has a name." On a miss, the definition of the name tapped ("Obtuse is between 90° and 180°."). On a hit, the definition of the right name. | All five named |
| 5 | Rule 1: straight line | Drags the red line along a straight line; both numbers change and the sum stays 180°. Taps Show me for the worked example | "Rule 1: angles on a straight line add up to 180°." "Drag the red line. They always make 180°." "76° + x = 180°" "So x = 180° − 76° = 104°." | The worked example is shown |
| 6 | Rule 2: opposite angles | Drags one of two crossing lines; the opposite angles stay equal. Taps Show me | "Rule 2: vertically opposite angles are equal." "The 68° angle is opposite x." "So x = 68°." "Angles next to each other still make 180°." | The worked example is shown |
| 7 | Watch out for traps | Answers two game-style questions (58°, then 88° on a straight line); every wrong answer explains its trap. Then the two habits appear | "The game hides traps in the answers." Traps: "That's the given angle. x is the other one." "That's 90° − 58°. A straight line is 180°, not 90°." "180° is the whole line. x is only part of it." Habits: "Estimate first: is x more or less than 90°?" "Then check: do they add up to 180°?" | Both questions answered |
| 8 | Read the radar | Taps each part of a full battle view (Cannon, Enemy, Given, x, Timer); the part lights up | "The bright line is your cannon." "The red line points at the enemy." "The orange angle is given." "The white angle is x. Find it!" "This bar is your time. Estimate first!" "Wrong answer? The enemy fires back." "Time runs out? The mission fails. Just retry the level." | All five parts tapped |
| 9 | Practice | Answers four questions in the game's format (two of each rule, new numbers, random orientations), with no time limit | GLIB taunts and reacts. Co-pilot: the rule on a miss, the second hint after another miss, the working on a hit ("64° + 116° = 180°"). | Four questions answered |
| 10 | Ready check | Sees the practice stars and the two rule cards; taps Launch mission (or Extra training) | "Perfect practice! You are ready, pilot." (or a gentler line for 1 to 2 stars) "Remember: estimate, then check." | Launch mission opens the game |
| + | Extra training (optional) | Drags the red line in three diagrams: a right angle split in two (90°), angles around a point (360°), a triangle (180°) | "The two parts of a right angle add up to 90°." "Angles around a point add up to 360°." "The three angles of a triangle add up to 180°." | Open-ended |

## How the lesson maps to the game

| Lesson scene | Prepares for, in the game |
|---|---|
| 2 What is an angle? | The cannon turning by the chosen answer in every battle |
| 3 Degrees | The radar's tick marks (10° and 30°); 180° and 90° as reference points for estimating |
| 4 Angle types | Ruling out answers: a reflex option (223°, 242°, 271°, 332° in L3, L4, L6, L8) can never be part of a straight-line pair |
| 5 Rule 1 | L1, L3, L4, L6, L8, the mastery levels M1 to M4 (mixed rules) and the Warden's questions |
| 6 Rule 2 | L2, L5, L7, M1 to M4 and the Warden's questions |
| 7 Traps | The wrong options in every level: the given angle itself (L1 76°, L3 118°, L4 43°, L6 152°), 180° (L2 to L8), the near miss (L8 89° and 91°) |
| 8 Read the radar | The battle view, the question bar and its time bar, the enemy firing back after a miss, the instant fail and Retry on a time-out |
| 9 Practice | The same question bar, capsules and feedback as a level; stars use the game's rule |
| 10 Ready check | Launch mission opens the game at its title screen |
| Extra training | The game's question generator also supports right-angle, around-a-point and triangle questions (src/game/angles.js), which the current levels do not use yet |

## Misconceptions, and how the lesson and game handle them

| Misconception | In the lesson | In the game |
|---|---|---|
| "x is the number I can see" (picking the given angle) | Traps scene, first wrong answer explains it; the white x arc and the orange given arc always use different colours | The given angle is always one of the options; a wrong pick turns the cannon to a visible miss |
| Using 90° instead of 180° for a straight line | Traps scene ("That's 90° − 58°…"); the Degrees scene shows the straight line as a half turn | Rule hint on a miss: "Angles on a straight line add up to 180°" |
| Choosing 180° itself | Traps scene ("180° is the whole line. x is only part of it.") | 180° is an option in levels L2 to L8 |
| For opposite angles, using 180° − given | Rule 2 scene shows the adjacent pair still makes 180° while the opposite pair stays equal; practice options include the supplement | L2 (112°), L5 (67°), L7 (133°) offer the supplement |
| Doubling the given angle | Practice options include the double (144°, 236°) with the rule hint on a miss | L2 (136°), L5 (226°), L7 (94°) offer the double |
| Accepting a close answer | Near-miss round (88° / 92°) and the check habit | L8 offers 89° and 91° |
| Guessing under time pressure | The estimate-first habit; practice has no time limit | The time bar turns amber then red, then the mission fails and the level restarts |

## Assessment

- During the lesson (formative): the angle-type sort, the two trap questions and the practice round all give immediate feedback in the comms box. Wrong answers explain the specific mistake rather than just marking it wrong.
- Practice result: the number of questions answered right on the first try out of 4 gives 1 to 3 stars, using the same rule as the game (all first try = 3 stars, at least 60% = 2, otherwise 1).
- In the game: stars for first-try answers every four levels, and a 3-star rating for the whole run on the victory screen.

## Accessibility

- Keyboard: → or Enter for Next, ← for Back, Space to finish or skip a line, Tab to reach every button. When the radar has focus (a round dashed ring shows), the arrow keys turn its line by 1° (Shift for 10°).
- Screen readers: each comms line is announced once in full (not letter by letter); the radar has a text description of the current angle.
- Colour is never the only cue: every arc carries its number or x, and the rules are written out.
- Motion: with the system's reduce-motion setting, animations and tweens are switched off.
- Layout: works from 390 px phones to large desktop screens, with no horizontal scrolling and nothing overlapping. The answer capsules, Back, Next and Launch mission are large touch targets (about 44 px tall or more); the smaller chips are spaced apart.
- Sound is optional; nothing is conveyed only by sound.

## Points added beyond the brief

These were not in the original request but fill gaps between the lesson and the game:

1. What an angle is, and degrees with the 90° / 180° / 360° landmarks, before any rule.
2. Angle types, including reflex, which helps rule out the game's 223° to 332° options.
3. A traps scene built from the game's real wrong options, with an explanation for each.
4. The two habits for the time limit: estimate first, then check the sum.
5. Reading the battle radar part by part, plus what happens on a miss and on a time-out.
6. A practice round in the game's exact format, with the raider and the game's feedback model and star rule.
7. A ready check with the two rules as cards, and the link that launches the game.
8. Optional extra training for the three other angle facts the game's code supports.
9. Keyboard, screen-reader and reduced-motion support.
10. This mapping between lesson scenes, game levels and misconceptions.

## Production notes

- Assets are copied from the game: the co-pilot, Warden and raider portraits (made with Figma AI), the answer capsules (POLYGON kit), the Next capsule and the cockpit art.
- The co-pilot's lines and the questions are in `src/scenes.js`; each scene is a small, self-contained block.
- The game link is `GAME_URL` in `src/main.js`.
- No build step: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules.

## Known limits and next steps

- No recorded voice-over yet; like the game, the lines use synthesized talk blips. Recorded character voices can be added per line once they exist.
- Progress is not saved between visits, and the practice result is not passed to the game.
- The lesson is not deployed yet; it can be published as its own static site, like the game.
